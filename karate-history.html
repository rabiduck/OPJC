Karate

History

From Okinawa to mainland Japan, the development of Shotokan, and the beginning of Old Priory Karate Club in York.

Origins

Karate's Okinawan roots.

Karate developed in Okinawa from local fighting traditions shaped over time by contact with Chinese martial arts and the culture of the Ryukyu Islands.

By the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, teachers in Okinawa were increasingly organising and teaching these methods in a more systematic way. Among the students of that generation was Gichin Funakoshi, who studied under prominent Okinawan teachers including Anko Azato and Anko Itosu.

Funakoshi

Karate reaches mainland Japan.

In 1922 Funakoshi demonstrated Okinawan Karate at a Ministry of Education exhibition in Tokyo, helping introduce the art to a much wider Japanese audience.

Judo founder Jigoro Kano then invited Funakoshi to demonstrate at the Kodokan. Funakoshi remained in Tokyo and continued teaching, playing a central role in the establishment of Karate on mainland Japan.

The dojo built for his students in 1939 became known as Shotokan, from Funakoshi's pen name Shoto. The name later became associated with the style itself.

Shotokan

A worldwide style.

Funakoshi's students continued developing and organising Shotokan after the Second World War. In 1948 former students formed the Japan Karate Association, with Funakoshi as its Supreme Master.

Shotokan subsequently spread internationally and today remains one of the most widely recognised styles of Karate, with clubs and organisations around the world maintaining their own traditions and approaches to training.

Old Priory Karate Club

Our story begins in 2025.

Old Priory Karate Club was founded in York in 2025 as a new addition to the Old Priory Martial Arts family.

The club teaches Shotokan Karate at the same Acomb dojo as Old Priory Judo Club and is affiliated with Bushido ZaZen International.

Unlike our Judo club's long history, this is a new chapter — and one we'll be able to expand as the Karate club grows.
